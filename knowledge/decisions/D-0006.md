# D-0006: Phase 4, purses, the For Sale block, trade between villagers, buying food

Accepted 2026-10-05. Phase 4 of the plan (part C of
`~/.claude/plans/the-following-requests-were-witty-church.md`): the spec's §11 and §12, and §2's
"no food and has emeralds → buys some". Rusty took the preview's one call ("Go with your
recommendation"). Built in two halves, as phases 1 and 2 were, each gated on its own:

- **4a (0.6.0):** a working market: purses, trades through them, the morning deposit, the hire fee
  paid to the villager, the For Sale block, shoppers buying what they need, and buying food.
- **4b (0.7.0):** climate, taste, wants, window shopping, and villagers selling to villagers.

## Rusty's call

- **The day-one purse.** Villagers already in the world start full, at the cap (64). That means
  every villager living on the box at the update, and those a village's structure places as new
  chunks generate. Villagers born, cured, hatched from an egg, summoned or spawned start at the
  deposit line (4), so a breeding pen can't make emeralds. A one-time cushion that
  runs out as it is spent; after it the purse rules. Rejected: everyone at 4 (selling to villagers
  dries up the day it ships), and a seeded start between 4 and 64.

## What the code it sits on does

Checked on 2026-10-05 in the NeoForge 21.1.248 sources and the box's mods folder.

- **A trade's result slot fills only for an offer that isn't sold out** (`MerchantContainer.
  updateSellItem`), and the client draws the red cross from the same flag, sent with the offers.
  The only other reader is `ShowTradesToPlayer`, which is cosmetic. So a purse closes a trade with a
  flag that lasts while a player trades, never touching the offer's uses: restocking and demand
  stay vanilla's.
- **NeoForge posts `TradeWithVillagerEvent` after every trade,** inside `notifyTrade`, before the
  container refills its result slot: the purse changes there, and a closed trade stays empty.
- **Vanilla's meeting runs only with a bell in reach** (`MEETING_POINT`). Shopping takes over the
  brain as D-0005's meal does instead. A free villager's social hours are vanilla's 9000–11000.
- **`Brain.addActivity` adds to an activity's behaviours** but replaces its conditions, so a
  behaviour added to vanilla's brain passes them again.
- **Every profession's price list,** vanilla and modded, is in `VillagerTrades.TRADES` once
  NeoForge's trade event has run. More Villagers' professions come in that way.
- **A villager in a newly generated chunk does not join as loaded from disk** (corrected during the
  build: the preview said it did). `addWorldGenChunkEntities` passes `false`; only a chunk read from
  its save passes `true`. What tells the cases apart is NeoForge's `FinalizeSpawnEvent`: a coremod
  sends every call to `Mob.finalizeSpawn` through it, with the spawn type: `STRUCTURE` for a
  village's people, `BREEDING`, `CONVERSION` (a cure, after the villager has joined), `SPAWN_EGG`,
  `COMMAND`, `SPAWNER` and the rest. A villager saved before purses joins with none.
- **Vanilla farmers carry their harvest** in their own 8 slots.
- **`setUnhappyCounter` is vanilla's head shake.**
- **Village Deed protects no blocks.** Warehouse Manager's D-0006 is the owner lock copied here.
- **The pack has no other economy mod.** Guard Villagers, More Villagers, Thief, Village Deed and
  Village Law are its villager mods.
- **Until now the hire fee was taken from the player and vanished.**

## The rule: 4a

**The purse.**
- Every villager has one: a whole number of emeralds from 0 to the cap (64), saved on it.
- Wandering traders have none and keep vanilla's trading.
- A villager's purse starts as Rusty's call above says: a structure's villager starts full as it is
  made; any other spawn starts at 4 as it is made (a cure, made after it joins, is set again then);
  and a villager joining with no purse at all (one saved before purses) gets a full one.

**Trading with players.** Every villager: free, hired or captive.
- A trade that pays the player emeralds needs them in the purse. Short, it shows sold out and its
  result slot stays empty.
- The emeralds a player pays (after any discount) go into the purse.
- Over the cap, the rest is lost; the sale is never refused.
- A trade that both pays and takes emeralds is netted: it needs what it pays less what it takes.
- The trade screen shows the villager's purse beside its title.

**The morning deposit.**
- A morning is 0 to 2000. The first time a villager is seen in a morning, if it is free or hired,
  grown and holds less than 4, it gets 2.
- Only while it is loaded: a villager loaded only later in the day missed that morning, and a
  missed morning is never paid back.
- Captives never get it.

**The hire fee** goes into the hired villager's purse.

**The For Sale block.**
- Crafted from a sign, a chest and an emerald.
- It belongs to whoever placed it. Only the owner opens or breaks it; explosions spare it.
- Its screen: what it sells (set by clicking the item onto its slot, components and all); how many a
  sale (1–64); the price of a sale (1–64 emeralds); 18 stock slots, which take that item only; 9
  proceeds slots, which only give; and the Ledger tab.
- Above it the item turns slowly. A player looking at it reads "8 Bread for 1 emerald", or
  "Sold out".
- It is closed when its stock is short of a sale or its proceeds can't take a sale's price.
- Hoppers, pipes and Create fill its stock (that item only) and take its proceeds.
- It is a point of interest, so villagers find it through the game's index.

**Who shops, and when.**
- Free and hired adults. Never captives, children, villagers in chains or on their way home.
- Once a social time, when it needs something and holds an emerald: a free villager from 9000 to
  11000, a hired worker from 8000 to 10000. A trip that came to nothing still counts.
- At stalls within 64 blocks of its bed. A villager whose bed is in a village bought through Village
  Deed shops only at the stalls of its owner and the players he trusts.
- Shopping takes over the brain as a meal does, and gives the schedule back.

**What it needs** (needs before wants; wants are 4b).
- **A free villager keeps a household:** what it has at home, as counts, saved on it.
  - Its profession's needs list (data, `serfdom/needs`) says how many of each it keeps and how many
    a day go: food for everyone; a farmer's hoe and bone meal; a butcher's raw meat and coal; and so
    on.
  - Each morning the household uses its share (no catch-up, as the deposit). Short of what it keeps
    is a need.
- **A hired worker needs:**
  - food, when the ready food in its home chest and the canteen fills less than a day (20 points);
    it goes to its home chest;
  - what its post shows it lacks: its tool, fuel, or the materials its stock rows are short of; it
    goes into its post's chests.
- Bought goods are carried home and only then put away. A trip cut short keeps what it carries
  (saved on the villager) and puts it away at its next chance.

**Which stall.** For its first need some stall in reach sells: the one with the lowest price an
item it can afford a sale of; if it can afford none, the cheapest (it finds out there). Ties to the
nearest. One customer at a time; the others wait close by up to 30 seconds, then try the next. At
most 3 sales a day.

**At the stall.**
- What it will pay for an item: its base value × 1.5 (a need).
- The price of an item: the price of a sale ÷ how many a sale.
  - At or under what it will pay: it buys.
  - Above: its chance falls in a straight line to nothing at 150% of what it will pay.
  - Its purse short of one sale: it can't afford it.
- It buys as many sales as cover its need, while it can pay and the stall has them, within the
  day's 3.
- Reactions: a bargain (an item at or under its base value) shows vanilla's green sparkles and its
  "yes"; any other purchase its "yes"; too pricey the head shake and "no"; can't afford an emerald
  held up, a "hmm", and away.

**Base value** (emeralds an item), the first that has one:
1. data (`serfdom/values`: items, then tags);
2. the median price an item over every offer of every profession's price list, bought or sold, at
   every level, read once the server has started (simple offers only: emeralds for items, or items
   for emeralds; a treasure map's search is never run);
3. food, at bread's value for each point it fills;
4. none: nobody wants it.

**Buying food.** The meal (D-0005) gains a step between raw food and nothing: buy the cheapest food
it will pay for at a stall in reach, enough to fill up, within the day's sales; eat there; take the
rest home. Captives never buy.

**The Ledger tab.** A line a visit (the day and hour, the villager's profession, what happened), the
last 64; and for each of the last 7 days: sold, too pricey, couldn't afford, not interested.

**Shown:** a Purse row on the Worker Screen.

**Settings:** `economy` [on] (off: vanilla trading, nobody shops, no deposit), `purse_cap` [64],
`deposit` [2], `deposit_below` [4], `shop_reach` [64], `sales_per_day` [3], `need_bonus` [1.5].

## The rule: 4b

- **Climate:** a village's from the biome at the villager's bed: hot (desert, savanna, badlands,
  jungle), cold (the snowy biomes), or temperate. Items tagged hot or cold; one from a climate not
  its own ×1.5. Untagged: no change.
- **Taste:** food, tools, decor and luxury (tags). A permanent multiplier 0.5–1.5 each, seeded from
  the villager's UUID, leaning by profession. Its highest is its favourite.
- **Wants:** anything with a value in a category it likes, up to a few of each; after needs; into
  its household or home chest.
- **Window shopping:** with emeralds and no need, it visits one stall in reach it hasn't seen today.
  Wants it: judges the price. Doesn't: a glance, and the ledger's "not interested". A purchase from
  its favourite category: vanilla's celebrate sound and a hop.
- **What it will pay:** base value × climate × taste × need.
- **Villagers selling:** a free villager sells what its profession makes from its own inventory, at
  base value: in practice a town farmer's harvest. The buyer walks over and pays it.

## Settled in the build: 4b

Built 2026-10-05 as 0.7.0, without a new preview, on Rusty's word. One line of the rule did not hold
as written, and its reading is below; the rest is detail the rule left open.

- **"With emeralds and no need" means no need anything in reach sells.** Read literally it never
  happens: every free villager's household starts short of food (it keeps 4) and of its trade's goods
  (a farmer's hoe and bone meal), and a hired worker is short whenever its post lacks something. In a
  market that sells none of those, nobody would ever window-shop, and wants would never matter. So a
  villager goes for its first need some stall or seller in reach sells, once a social time (4a's
  rule, unchanged; the trip counts whether or not one does), and otherwise looks. Needs still come
  first.
- **Window shopping repeats through the social time, one stall at a time,** each to the nearest open
  stall it may use that it has not seen today, until it has seen them all; that is what "hasn't seen
  today" is for. Every stall it goes to is seen for the day, a need's and a meal's too. The day's three
  sales still cap what it buys.
- **At a look, an item that meets a need is judged as a need** (with the need's bonus) before it is
  judged for taste. Otherwise it is not interested, a glance of half a second.
- **A want** is up to `wants_each` (3) of one item, counted where it would go: a free villager's
  household, a worker's home chest. A worker without a home chest, or whose chest has no room for one,
  wants nothing (the spec's "no purchase if the home chest is full"). Wants are never used up unless a
  need uses them (food).
- **Taste:** each category's multiplier is 1 - s + 2s·u^(2^-lean), s the spread (0.5), u a draw in
  [0, 1) made by SplitMix64's finalizer over the UUID and the category, written out in the domain so it
  never changes with the JDK. A lean is -1 to 1, from the profession's file (`"taste"`), everyone's and
  its own added. Nothing is saved: the taste is drawn again when asked, so a profession change leans it
  anew. It likes a category above 1; its favourite is the highest, ties in the order food, tools, decor,
  luxury; an item in two categories goes by the higher.
- **Climate:** biome tags `#serfdom:climate/hot` (`#c:is_desert`, `#minecraft:is_savanna`,
  `#minecraft:is_badlands`, `#minecraft:is_jungle`) and `#serfdom:climate/cold` (`#c:is_snowy`); a
  biome in both or neither is temperate. Items: `#serfdom:climate/hot` and `/cold`. An item tagged both
  is foreign only to a temperate village. No bed: the biome where it stands.
- **Categories:** `#serfdom:taste/food` (NeoForge's `c:foods` without raw meat, raw fish and food
  poisoning), `/tools` (`c:tools`), `/decor`, `/luxury`.
- **Selling:** the profession's file names what it sells and how many of each it keeps (`"sells"`).
  The farmer sells bread (keeping 3, the 12 food points vanilla breeding wants), potatoes and carrots
  (keeping 8 each to plant) and beetroot. Purses hold whole emeralds, so a seller sells in lots: an
  item worth an emerald or more singly at its value rounded; a cheaper one as many as round to an
  emerald's worth, for one (bread: six). Short of a lot, no sale. A seller is free, grown, awake and not
  trading with a player; it serves one buyer at a time; its purse past the cap loses the rest. Sellers
  serve needs, never a look, and a worker's meal still buys only at stalls, as 4a has it.
- **Checked in vanilla 1.21.1:** a farmer makes bread from its own wheat (`WorkAtComposter.makeBread`,
  while it has 36 or fewer); every villager picks up bread, potatoes, carrots, beetroot, wheat and their
  seeds (`Villager.WANTED_ITEMS`); and a farmer with 24 food points or more throws half its food to
  other villagers for nothing (`TradeWithVillager`). That sharing goes on beside the selling.
- **Found and fixed:** a For Sale block counted as storage (its handler has 27 slots), so a stall
  beside a worker's bed was its home chest, and one in a post's area took the post's harvest into its
  stock. Storage now leaves stalls out.
- **Found and fixed:** villagers planned their way straight over a For Sale block. Vanilla's
  `isPathfindable` calls any block whose collision is no full block open ground, and a mob plans to
  jump onto any block it can't walk through unless its path type is a fence; the stall's sign makes
  its top a full block high, within a villager's jump, but too narrow to stand on past the sign. A
  window shopper bound for the next stall stood stuck on the one between (2 of 8 runs of the gate;
  its trace: a 12-step straight path, "can't reach"). The stall now says it is no open ground and
  is a fence (NeoForge's `getBlockPathType`), so paths go around it and end beside it, which is
  where a shopper's 2.5 blocks of arrival were always met. The Work Post was given the same and
  taken back: workers' arrival at their post leans on reaching above its pole, and 1a's gate test
  failed with it (a farmer stopped in the gateway). A villager can stand on a pole; it can't on the
  stall's sign edge.

Settings: `climate_bonus` [1.5], `taste_spread` [0.5], `wants_each` [3].

## Settled by the spec, the role or a past decision

- **Workers never sell their owner's goods.** What is in his posts' chests is his; they are
  customers, never sellers. He sells through stalls, and his workers shop there like anyone.
- **A free villager's purchases go to its household,** not a chest: town houses rarely have one,
  and the spec wants "stocking what a village's professions use" to sell reliably.
- **A captive keeps its purse** and trades with players through it, but gets no deposit and never
  shops.
- **A hungry worker buys during its meal,** not at social time: hunger can't wait for 8000.
- **Chat stays vanilla's meeting,** untouched.

## Rejected

- **Everyone at 4 on day one, and a seeded start:** Rusty, above.
- **Workers selling from their post's chests,** as "villagers earn by selling" might read: those are
  the owner's goods.
- **A shopping trip inside vanilla's meeting:** it never runs without a bell.
- **Marking a closed trade by using it up** (`setToOutOfStock`): it would restock and shift demand
  as a real sale does.
