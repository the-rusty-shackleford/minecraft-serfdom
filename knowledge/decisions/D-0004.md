# D-0004: Phase 2b, equipment slots and the villager armour layer

Accepted 2026-10-04. The second half of phase 2 (part C of
`~/.claude/plans/the-following-requests-were-witty-church.md`; 2a is [D-0003](D-0003.md)): the
spec's §5 "owned villagers can wear armor and clothes … renders on the villager". Rusty took every
recommendation of the preview ("Lets go with your recommendations for all").

## Rusty's calls

- **Leggings take the robe off.** A villager's robe hangs to 3.5 pixels above its feet, so
  leggings worn under it would show nowhere above the boots. Vanilla's zombie villager just lets its
  robe hide them. With anything on its legs, the villager's robe is not drawn, and its tunic and the
  leggings show, as on Guard Villagers' guards. A chestplate alone sits over the robe. The cost: a
  worker in leggings no longer shows its trade's robe.
- **Armour wears as on a player.** Vanilla never wears a mob's armour. Without wear, one diamond set
  would keep a worker nearly unkillable for good. With it, armour is upkeep the blacksmith's stock
  list can feed.
- **Gear on a villager that leaves alive.** Set free from the screen, it takes everything off and
  drops it where it stands, with its tool. Escaping, or freed by the law, it leaves wearing it. The
  piece stays marked to drop whole, so recapturing or killing it gets the gear back. With the law,
  that loss is part of the price of being caught, as the confiscated chain is.

## What the code it sits on does

Checked on 2026-10-04 in the NeoForge 21.1.248 sources and the pack's jars.

- **Vanilla.**
  - Every mob, a villager among them, has four armour slots. Armour in them already protects:
    attribute modifiers and enchantments act on any living entity.
  - A dispenser already puts armour on a villager. `ArmorItem.dispenseArmor` equips any mob that
    picks up loot, and villagers do. It marks the slot's drop chance 2.0: above 1 means "always
    drops, undamaged". It was invisible only because `VillagerRenderer` has no armour layer.
  - A mob's armour never wears. `LivingEntity.hurtArmor` and `hurtHelmet` are empty, and only
    `Player` fills them in, through `doHurtEquipment`. NeoForge routes that through
    `CommonHooks.onArmorHurt`, which works for any entity.
  - A zombie's kill converts a villager with `convertTo(ZOMBIE_VILLAGER, false)`, which deletes
    its equipment.
  - `Mob.dropCustomDeathLoot` drops a piece whose chance is above 1 whole, unless it has Curse of
    Vanishing, and only with `doMobLoot` on. NeoForge's `LivingDropsEvent` fires either way.
  - The villager model:
    - head 8×10×8, with a hat overlay at +0.51;
    - body 8×12×6, with the robe (`jacket`) 8×20×6 at +0.5;
    - the arms one folded part pitched −0.75 about (0, 3, −1): two 4×8×4 upper arms and a 8×4×4
      forearm block.
  - Vanilla's zombie villager lifts the helmet 2 pixels.
  - `VillagerModel.hatVisible` sets head, hat and rim at once. `VillagerProfessionLayer` calls it
    on every draw.
  - The armour models' boxes come from `HumanoidModel.createMesh`: deformation 1.0 outer, 0.5
    inner, and 0.1 less on the legs.
  - `HumanoidArmorLayer` needs a `HumanoidModel` parent. It copies the parent's part poses into the
    armour model, scales included (`ModelPart.copyFrom`).
- **NeoForge.** `IClientItemExtensions.getGenericArmorModel` swaps in a mod's own armour model and
  copies only the original's part poses and scales into it, never its boxes.
- **Lucky's Wardrobe 2.0.0** (the pack's clothes; sha1 `a0a37d72`, on the box and in the client
  pack):
  - It has 50 items. All are `ArmorItem`s, each drawn by its own player-shaped model.
  - The hats and aprons (`CosmeticItem`) cannot be damaged and give no armour.
  - The outfit pieces (27 `OutfitArmorItem`s: the taiga, snowy, desert, savanna, jungle, swampy and
    entertainer's sets) are built with no durability, so they never wear. They do give armour
    points: the taiga boots give 1, as the GameTest logs.
  - The snowy hood is the one `HoodArmorItem`.
  - Its client code needs Curios to load.

## The rule

**The slots.** The Worker Screen is now a menu (`WorkerMenu`). The worker is drawn beside its
head, chest, legs and feet slots, with the player's inventory below.
- **Who:** only the owner of a living villager in their level and within 8 blocks
  (`Wardrobe.access`). The server asks on every click and every tick, so a screen left open on a
  villager that dies, goes free or is led off takes nothing.
- **What fits:** a slot takes one piece that may be worn there, by the item's own say
  (`canEquip`). That covers armour, the turtle shell, a carved pumpkin, heads, the elytra and
  Lucky's Wardrobe's clothes.
- **Binding:** Curse of Binding keeps a piece on, but not for a player in creative.
- **Shift-clicks:** they move a piece into its first empty slot. A worn piece goes back to the
  inventory, then the hotbar. What has no slot moves between inventory and hotbar
  (`Wardrobe.route`).
- **Whose slots:** the slots are the villager's own, so what it wears is vanilla's armour. A piece
  put on is marked to drop whole (`Parting.GUARANTEED`, as the dispenser does).
- **Which villagers:** hired workers, captives, cuffed villagers and children alike. Workers never
  dress themselves.
- **The screen:**
  - It opens with vanilla's open packet. The worker's details follow as the existing view payload,
    which binds the client's menu.
  - It fits 688×288 GUI pixels: Rusty's 3440×1440 at GUI scale 5 (`WorkerLayout`).
- **Protection** is vanilla's.

**Wear** (`VillagerWearMixin`), on every villager:
- A hit takes max(1, damage ÷ 4) durability from each piece.
- A falling block wears the helmet.
- A piece that cannot be hurt (Unbreakable, the clothes) does not wear.
- A spent piece breaks off.

**Parting** (`Parting.fate`):

| Way | What it wore |
|---|---|
| Dies | Drops with its tool, load and chain; a vanishing piece vanishes |
| Turns zombie | The same (the game would delete it) |
| Set free from the screen | Drops where it stands |
| Escapes | Stays on |
| Freed by the law | Stays on |

**The layer** (`VillagerArmourLayer`), on every villager, owned or not:
- It draws through vanilla's `HumanoidArmorLayer`, so dyes, trims, the glint and mods' own models
  and textures work.
- The armour sits on a stand-in `HumanoidModel` whose parts are posed from the villager's model
  each frame (`Fit`):
  - The helmet is lifted 2 pixels in the head's own frame. Its pivot is moved by the head's
    rotation of (0, −2, 0), so it turns about the head's pivot however the head turns.
  - The chest is 1.32× deeper, covering the robe's top.
  - The sleeves sit on the crossed upper arms at 0.7 of their length, shifted −0.8 along them. The
    folded forearms stay bare, so the cuffs show.
  - The legs swing with the villager's.
- The fit lies wholly in poses and scales, because that is all NeoForge passes to a mod's own
  armour model. So Lucky's Wardrobe's clothes fit as armour does.
- `FitTest` checks each cover by at least 0.25 pixels against both models' boxes. The booth's
  photographs judge the look.
- **Dress:** with anything on the legs the robe is not drawn. With anything on the head the hat
  and rim are hidden (`VillagerModelMixin`, after the profession layer sets them).
- **Elytra:** vanilla's layer, hung 1.5 pixels farther back. A child's is not shrunk twice.
- **The portrait:** the need icon is not drawn over it.

## Rejected

- **Leggings under the robe.** True to the robe, but leggings would never show.
- **Plates over the robe's skirt, drawn from the leggings' texture.** It is new geometry that
  Lucky's Wardrobe's own models cannot share, so its trousers would still vanish.
- **Vanilla's mob rule, armour forever:** Rusty, above.
- **Every way out drops the gear, or every way out keeps it:** Rusty, above.
- **Lifting the helmet in the armour's box coordinates,** as vanilla's zombie-villager armour model
  does. A mod's own model keeps its boxes, so its hats would sink 2 pixels into the head.
- **Opening the menu with NeoForge's extra-data packet.** It cannot be sent to a GameTest's mock
  player, so no GameTest could open the screen the way a player does. The view payload carries the
  same data right after vanilla's open packet.
- **Workers dressing themselves from the chests.** The spec gives the slots to the owner. Phase 5
  arms workers for raids with weapons only, and worn armour stays on.
